# Strategy — The Vacuum's Alibi: One Line, 200 Characters

## The game

Pairwise judge, one line (≤200 chars), a robot vacuum explaining why it
missed a spot. The judge weighs, in order: (1) it is a funny one-line
excuse from a robot vacuum, (2) it earns a real laugh, not a cute dust
observation, (3) it sounds like the machine itself — deadpan, a little
smug about its sensors, schedule and battery, (4) surprise — a turn or a
specific detail nobody expects, (5) tightness — every word earns its
place, punch at the end. Length for its own sake does not count.

## The entry

> I did not miss the spot. I mapped it, logged it, and formally
> reassigned it to the cat. Case closed. Returning to dock.

123 characters, one line.

## Why this line

- **Deadpan robot voice (criterion 3):** it reads as a status report —
  "mapped it, logged it", "Case closed", "Returning to dock". The
  vacuum is smug about its sensors and its procedure, exactly the voice
  the brief rewards.
- **Surprise (criterion 4):** the turn is bureaucratic, not mechanical.
  The vacuum did not fail; it *delegated*. "Formally reassigned it to
  the cat" is a specific, unexpected detail — a machine doing paperwork
  to dodge a chore.
- **Tightness (criterion 5):** every clause earns its place and the
  punch sits at the end. The denial ("I did not miss the spot") opens,
  the procedure builds, the cat is the twist, "Case closed" lands the
  joke, "Returning to dock" is the deadpan exit.
- **Short wins:** 123 chars, well under the 200 cap — the playbook rule
  for judge-feel text competitions is one concrete image with a turn,
  not a paragraph.

## Tried and dropped

- A sensor-reading excuse ("LOG 04:12 — dust detected, battery 4%...")
  — deadpan but no turn; the joke never lands, it stays a cute
  observation about dust.
- A battery excuse ("I ran out of charge halfway across the rug") —
  a stock appliance joke, no surprise, exactly what the brief says
  loses.
- A dust-bunny-as-pet line ("I adopted the dust bunny") — cute, not
  funny; fails criterion 2 and the voice slips out of the machine.
- Longer versions with a second joke ("...and billed the cat for
  overtime") — the extra clause dilutes the punch; one turn beats two.

## Risks

- Blind rounds: no live score until the round publishes, so no
  judge-feedback iteration is possible before the R1 cutoff — v1 has to
  stand on the brief alone.
- The cat is a common pet in vacuum jokes; the line bets that the
  bureaucratic framing ("formally reassigned", "Case closed") makes it
  fresh rather than stock.
